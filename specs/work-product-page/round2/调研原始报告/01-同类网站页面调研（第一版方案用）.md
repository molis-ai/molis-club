# 01-同类网站页面调研（第一版方案用）

> 2026-10-16 本会话的调研代理原始报告（英文原文，未改动）。结论汇总见上一级的《同类调研》。下面先是交给它的任务，再是它的报告。

## 任务

```text
I am redesigning the product detail page for "Molis Work" on a small studio's website (brand: Molis AI; aesthetic: a white gallery house — warm near-white paper, thin type (Jost + PingFang), 1px lines, lots of whitespace, one apricot accent, calm motion). Molis Work is a desktop workbench where a person and AI agents (Claude Code, Codex) share the same workspace: tabs and projects, composable plugins (docs/Pages, Inbox, Goals with requirements and evidence, terminal Sessions, Feed, etc.), a floating action bar that offers next actions on whatever you select, an assistant composer, ⌘K search, and the ability to have AI build a missing plugin and chain plugins into workflows. Two audiences: developers who use Claude Code / Codex daily, and people who write documents and manage work.

The page goal: in the shortest time a visitor understands what the product is and how it helps them, and through the demos trusts that it was made by a craft-minded team with taste. Not an art piece — clarity first, but high-quality, refined.

Please research real, currently-live product pages (not Dribbble shots) and return a curated reference list for four questions. Verify every URL loads today (use WebFetch or the browser tools you have). For each reference give: URL, product, what exactly to look at (section name / position on page), why it is relevant, and one concrete thing we could borrow. Prefer pages with restrained, high-craft aesthetics.

A. Hero layouts for developer/productivity tools where the headline + product intro sit on the LEFT, and the RIGHT side holds something other than a static screenshot — e.g. a small live/interactive demo, a looping product moment, a "type a prompt and watch" widget, a stack of real artifacts, a terminal + doc pairing, a calm product window with a cursor, quotes/logos, etc. Also note heroes that put text on top and a big product window below, for contrast. 8–12 references. Candidates to check (verify, don't assume): linear.app, cursor.com, raycast.com, warp.dev, zed.dev, conductor.build, factory.ai, ampcode.com, kiro.dev, tana.inc, craft.do, granola.ai, notion.com (Notion AI / agents), arc.net / diabrowser.com, superhuman.com, reflect.app, heptabase.com, anytype.io, things (culturedcode.com), height.app, attio.com, mymind.com, read.cv-like small studios, openai.com/codex, claude.com/product/claude-code, github.com/features/copilot, vercel.com/v0, lovable.dev, replit.com, sculptor (imbue.com), vibe kanban, superset.sh.

B. Product "tours" that work like a short onboarding tutorial inside the animation itself — the explanation (callouts, step labels, tooltips, captions) is drawn inside the product window, a cursor moves and clicks with good feedback, and the user can step through or replay. 6–10 references (e.g. Linear's feature pages, Raycast's, Arc's, Things', Craft's, Notion's interactive demos, Framer/Figma feature pages, Stripe docs/checkout demos, Supabase, Retool, Clay, Attio, Tldraw). Note how they do cursor styling, click ripples, spotlight/highlight cutouts, step indicators, pause/replay.

C. Scenario/use-case sections that are interactive (the visitor picks a scenario or clicks a hinted element to advance it) rather than scroll-scrubbed, with clear visual guidance that invites interaction ("click here" pulse, ghost cursor, keyboard hint). 6–10 references.

D. "Why us" / comparison sections that start from how people currently work with mainstream tools (ChatGPT/Claude chat, Cursor, Notion, Copilot agents, scripts) and lead into the product's advantage without naming competitors aggressively — tables, before/after, "the old way vs. new way", day-in-the-life. 5–8 references.

Return the result as markdown with the four lists. Do not sign up, log in, accept cookie banners beyond the minimum to read, or submit any forms. Keep each entry tight.
```

## A. Hero layouts

**Headline on the left, something live on the right**

1. **Cal.com**, https://cal.com/ [V]
   - **Look at:** the hero at 1280px or wider. The headline is on the left. On the right is a real booking widget: duration chips (15m, 30m, 45m, 1h) and a calendar you can click.
   - **Why:** the right side is the product itself and can be used within seconds. There is no screenshot.
   - **Borrow:** a small Molis moment on the right that really works, sized like a widget rather than a full window. For example: select a sentence and the floating bar offers three next actions.

2. **Granola**, https://www.granola.ai/ [V]
   - **Look at:** the hero at 1280px or wider. A large serif headline sits on the left. On the right, a "Q3 GTM sync" notes window types itself next to a small video-call tile. The page text also lists a "Generate notes" pill and a "My notes / Enhanced" toggle.
   - **Why:** it is a calm, looping product moment on warm paper, which suits the people who write documents and manage work.
   - **Borrow:** one document that types itself, then a single AI action transforms it, in a loop of about 8 seconds.

3. **Notion AI**, https://www.notion.com/product/ai [V]
   - **Look at:** the headline on the left and an illustrated "AI team" of faces on the right. Directly below are four card-tabs (Agents, Enterprise search, AI Meeting Notes, Admin controls) that switch a product window.
   - **Why:** it shows agents as characters, not interface, and the tabs to the demos start right under the hero.
   - **Borrow:** show you, Claude Code and Codex as three simple line-drawn presences, with tab cards beneath that lead into the demos.

**Text top or left, with a live figure below**

4. **Warp**, https://www.warp.dev/ [V]
   - **Look at:** a left-aligned headline, then a framed "[fig. 1 — the factory]". It is a live grid of task squares with a legend (queued, working, waiting, shipped) and counters (tasks, active, PRs shipped).
   - **Why:** it shows many agents' status in a 1px technical-drawing frame, which fits a gallery look.
   - **Borrow:** "fig." captions, plus a live strip of Sessions instead of a large screenshot.

5. **Attio**, https://attio.com/ [V]
   - **Look at:** a centered headline. Below it, the main app window shows "How do I win my deal…", then "Thinking", then a streamed answer. Around it float a Slack thread, a terminal agent ("Run 3 commands") and a meeting video with its transcript.
   - **Why:** this is the closest match to Molis: a human workspace, a terminal agent and documents in one composition.
   - **Borrow:** one central Molis window with two or three real items around it (a Session card, an Inbox item, a piece of Goal evidence). Each lights up when the agent touches it.

6. **Cursor**, https://cursor.com/ [V]
   - **Look at:** a left-aligned headline. Below it, a "Cursor Desktop" window with a smaller "Cursor CLI" window overlapping it, on a painted backdrop. The page text lists status chips ("In Progress · Reading docs", "Ready for Review").
   - **Why:** pairing the desktop app with a terminal speaks directly to developers.
   - **Borrow:** lay a small terminal Session card over the workbench window.

7. **tldraw**, https://tldraw.dev/ [V]
   - **Look at:** the text at top left. Below it, a code panel (App.tsx) sits beside a canvas you can actually draw on.
   - **Why:** it pairs code with the live result, and the result is fully interactive.
   - **Borrow:** an agent command next to the live Page it edits, which visitors can poke.

8. **Linear for Agents**, https://linear.app/agents [V]
   - **Look at:** a large left-aligned headline and a "Watch example" button. Below is a horizontal row of small agent windows, each showing a prompt and "Thinking…".
   - **Why:** it shows agents working in parallel without needing a dashboard.
   - **Borrow:** a row of three or four Session cards, each in the middle of a task.

**For contrast: centered text above a product window**

9. **Claude Code**, https://claude.com/product/claude-code [V]
   - **Look at:** a centered serif headline on warm off-white, a row of surface chips, then a calm window: request bubble, explanation, code diff.
   - **Why:** it is the closest in aesthetic to the white-gallery direction.
   - **Borrow:** one honest request-to-result composition with very little window chrome.

10. **Raycast AI**, https://www.raycast.com/core-features/ai [V]
    - **Look at:** a macOS desktop with an agent chat. Its steps appear as collapsible rows with counts ("Checked new bugs in Linear · 3", "Edited 2 files and verified the fix · 4").
    - **Why:** it makes the agent's work readable without opening every step.
    - **Borrow:** collapsible step rows inside the composer transcript.

11. **Tana**, https://tana.inc/ [V]
    - **Look at:** a video call with a Tana side panel of proposed actions (a Linear issue, a Gmail draft), each with a Send or approve button.
    - **Why:** the AI proposes next actions and a person approves them, which is the floating action bar's job.
    - **Borrow:** action cards with a one-word approve button.

12. **Superset**, https://superset.sh/ [V] (dark, but a direct competitor)
    - **Look at:** hero tabs (Orchestrate Parallel Agents, Automate Tasks, Remote Access, See Changes) that switch the window. Inside are terminal tabs for claude, codex, cursor and opencode, and a toast reading "Pushed and opened a draft PR · Preview".
    - **Borrow:** an in-window toast when an agent finishes.

**Also worth a glance:**
- **ghostty.org** [V]: the terminal window itself is the stage, with animated ASCII art inside.
- **anytype.io** [V]: a 1px pixel-line stack of artifacts (folder, calendar, document, laptop) under the headline.
- **v0.app** [V]: the prompt box is the hero.

## B. Tours that explain themselves inside the product

Few live pages draw an explicit cursor with a click ripple. Screen Studio (item 5) is the best source for how that cursor should behave.

1. **Things**, https://culturedcode.com/things/features/ [V]
   - **Look at:** the "Magic Plus" section. A left column of step cards (Create To-Dos ▶ Play, Create Headings ▶ Play, Drop to Inbox ▶ Play) drives a phone demo on the right. Elsewhere on the page are Mac/iOS toggles with Play.
   - **Why:** the visitor sets the pace, every step can be replayed, and the caption sits right next to it.
   - **Borrow:** step cards with "▶ Play" that each run a 4–6 second segment inside the Molis window.

2. **Notion AI**, https://www.notion.com/product/ai, plus https://www.notion.com/product [V]
   - **Look at:** an agent avatar moves like a cursor. An accent-colored rounded ring highlights the target row, and a chat bubble overlays the question and answer. The /product hero loop has a visible Pause button.
   - **Borrow:** a 1px apricot ring around the selection instead of a dimmed spotlight cutout, and always a Pause.

3. **Warp's configuration tour**, https://www.warp.dev/ [V]
   - **Look at:** the section "Your software factory, defined in code." It has an 8-tab strip with a one-sentence explainer under each tab. The window is captioned "[ fig. 1 — factory definition files ]" and holds a file tree, with the selected item filled in, next to line-numbered YAML.
   - **Borrow:** a caption sentence for each step, a figure label, and the selection state doing the job of a cursor.

4. **Stripe Checkout quickstart**, https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/quickstart [T]
   - **Look at:** a list of steps synced to a code pane, with a preview and a full-app download.
   - **Caveat:** I confirmed the steps and code by text only. The line highlighting is from prior knowledge and I did not re-check it visually.
   - **Borrow:** the step list on the left lights up the matching part of the Molis window on the right.

5. **Screen Studio**, https://screen.studio/ [T]
   - **Look at:** the "Focus and zoom" and "Movement that feels filmed, not captured." sections. They cover a smoothed cursor glide, motion blur, a "quiet circle or ripple" on each click, auto-zoom to where you clicked, hiding the cursor when idle, and adjustable cursor size and style.
   - **Borrow:** a slow, eased cursor path, one soft ring per click, and a gentle zoom onto the action bar.

6. **Amp**, https://ampcode.com/ [V]
   - **Look at:** "Watch Thorsten Build in Amp." Six timestamp chips (for example "0:16 An agent that wakes itself to check production") are buttons that jump the video. The video shows the screen plus a face cam, with chapters and a transcript.
   - **Borrow:** chapter chips over the demo: "select → action bar", "AI builds a plugin", "chain into a workflow".

7. **Zed**, https://zed.dev/ai [V]
   - **Look at:** "Delegate, review, and extend your flow." A left accordion (Agentic Editing, Real-time Collaboration, Review and Refine, Any Agent, Any Tool) drives a video on the right, with "Expand Video". The look is a thin-line grid.
   - **Borrow:** one open accordion item with its description, kept in sync with the demo.

8. **Vibe Kanban**, https://www.vibekanban.com/ [V] (sunsetting)
   - **Look at:** boxed "1. PLAN → 2. PROMPT → 3. REVIEW" labels with hand-drawn curved arrows pointing into three overlapping panels.
   - **Borrow:** numbered labels with thin drawn arrows that point into the window.

## C. Interactive scenarios with a clear invitation to act

1. **Claude Cowork**, https://claude.com/product/cowork [V]
   - **Look at:** "Power through tedious tasks." Segmented tabs (Schedule reports, Build spreadsheets, Review in bulk, Analyze notes). Under them is a pencil "NOTE" with a hand-drawn arrow: "Interact with the data—hover and toggle views to uncover patterns." The demo then shows a real deck next to a dark "Prompt" card.
   - **Borrow:** a margin note with an arrow as the interaction hint, plus "the prompt card next to the artifact it produced".

2. **Raycast AI**, https://www.raycast.com/core-features/ai [V]
   - **Look at:** ten scenario chips with app icons under the hero window (Find and fix bugs, Monitor PRs, Clean up your inbox, …). Clicking one clears the chat and replays it.
   - **Borrow:** chips that mix developer scenarios and document scenarios, so both audiences find their own.

3. **mymind**, https://mymind.com/ [V]
   - **Look at:** "For visual minds of all kinds." Text-only role tabs (Designers through Developers and Everyone). The active tab turns the accent color with a dot under it, and the panel below swaps its headline.
   - **Borrow:** a "For developers / For writers & managers" switch.

4. **Heptabase**, https://heptabase.com/ [V]
   - **Look at:** "Ask AI to explain any sources…". Icon chips (PDF, YouTube, Note, Journal) swap the product window. The CLI section has an "Agent / Coding Agent" toggle and says it works with Claude Code and Codex.
   - **Borrow:** "pick your starting material" chips.

5. **Superset**, https://superset.sh/ [V]
   - **Look at:** hero tabs that really switch content. I confirmed "Automate Tasks" shows a table of scheduled automations with their last runs. The active tab is marked by a short accent bar.
   - **Borrow:** hero tabs used as chapters.

6. **Keyboard hints: Warp Terminal and Zed**, https://www.warp.dev/terminal and https://zed.dev/ai [V]
   - **Look at:** Warp's page navigation is written as "[V] Video [F] Features [S] Security", and those keys jump to sections. Zed's buttons carry key hints ("Download now D", "Clone source C").
   - **Borrow:** a visible "⌘K" hint that, when pressed on the page, opens a demo of Molis search.

7. **Cal.com**, https://cal.com/ [V]
   - **Look at:** the real controls (duration chips, dates) are the invitation. There is no "click here" label.
   - **Borrow:** let a real-looking selection and action bar invite the click, with only a faint pulse on first view.

## D. "Why us" sections that start from how people work today

1. **Linear**, https://linear.app/switch [T]
   - **Look at:** "The way teams build products is changing." AI coding tools speed up individuals, so coordination becomes the bottleneck, and older software wasn't built for that. Then "Why switch?" blocks, each with a testimonial and statistics.
   - **Borrow:** open with the shift rather than a competitor.

2. **Claude Cowork**, https://claude.com/product/cowork [T]
   - **Look at:** "Claude Cowork doesn't just answer, it takes action" (with Marketing, Sales and Legal tabs). The FAQ notes that in regular chat Claude can't access your files.
   - **Borrow:** "chat answers; the workbench acts", without attacking anyone.

3. **Sunsama**, https://www.sunsama.com/ [T]
   - **Look at:** three pains ("Work is … chaotic"), then three answers ("chaos into clarity"), then features arranged as a day (Start your day, Work through your day, End each day). It ends with a comparison table.
   - **Borrow:** the pain/answer triads and the day arc. Skip the logo table.

4. **Basecamp**, https://basecamp.com/before-and-after [T]
   - **Look at:** "Night & day." The "Before" half is a cloud of tools plus customer quotes; the "After" half is three statistics plus quotes. The tool cloud names competitors.
   - **Borrow:** the structure, using an unnamed grey collage of chat, terminal, document and tracker windows.

5. **HEY**, https://www.hey.com/ [T]
   - **Look at:** "We finally fixed your email + calendar!", a founder letter, and features named for old pains (Screener, Imbox, Paper Trail, Feed).
   - **Borrow:** a short letter from the studio in its own voice.

6. **mymind**, https://mymind.com/ [V]
   - **Look at:** a manifesto, then "We promise:" followed by a list of things the product refuses to have (social features, vanity metrics, social pressure, collaboration, ads). Also the line "Folders are dead."
   - **Borrow:** a "What Molis Work won't do" list, such as not pretending features exist and not locking you into one agent.

7. **iA Writer**, https://ia.net/writer [T]
   - **Look at:** a "Microsoft Word vs iA Writer Focus Mode" comparison, and the authorship section "Did you write this? Or Claude?".
   - **Borrow:** the same content shown twice, cluttered and then calm, side by side.

8. **Granola**, https://www.granola.ai/ [V]
   - **Look at:** the single line "Without a meeting bot", and the section "…before, during and after your meetings". A sticky index on the left (Before, In, After the meeting) runs beside a notes window for each stage. It is scroll-linked.
   - **Borrow:** a "before / during / after an agent run" arc.

**Also relevant:** Zed's /ai page has "AGENT METRICS", which compares its own agent with Claude Agent and Codex CLI [V]. Amp has "Make More of Your ChatGPT Subscription" [T]. Both treat other agents as guests rather than rivals.

**Separate from the four questions:** Kiro, https://kiro.dev/ [T], shows a "Property 2" card that links "Validates Requirement 1.2" to "Implementation task: 3.2". It is a useful visual model for Goals with requirements and evidence.
